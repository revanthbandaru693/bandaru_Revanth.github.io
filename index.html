About & experience

Where I've trained, and the kind of work I'm chasing next.

I got into games the way a lot of environment artists do — by taking apart the worlds I liked playing in to understand why they felt believable. That curiosity turned into a BSc (Hons) in Computer Science and Game Development at ICAT Design and Media College, where I split my time between greyboxing levels, painting terrain, and wiring up UI that actually survives contact with a player.

Most of my recent work sits at the intersection of interface and environment: training simulators that need a HUD a first-time user can read in seconds, and 3D spaces — Indian village streets, military terrain, driving routes — that need to feel lived-in rather than assembled. I care as much about how a menu reads on a touchscreen kiosk as I do about how light falls across a mud wall.

I'm applying for a Master's in Game Design to push both sides further — sharper UX thinking, stronger environment craft — and to work alongside people who'll argue with my decisions. Long term, I want to be the artist a small team trusts with both the world and the interface that sits on top of it.
